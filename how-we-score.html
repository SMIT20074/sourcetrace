analytics Provenance Intelligence Methodology

Objective, Non-Evaluative Sourcing Metrics.

SourceTrace does not evaluate whether a claim is "true" or "false." Instead, our algorithms calculate a transparent Provenance Confidence Score based strictly on four verifiable sourcing signals.

The Four Core Backend Components

domain

1. Source Track Record

Measures disclosed source metadata facts from our media registry — including founding date, ownership structure, parent organization, and documented correction history policies.

Principle: Based on objective registry data, not subjective editorial opinions of news outlets.
verified

2. Cross-Verification

Calculates the number of genuinely independent news organizations reporting on the same core event, separate from wire-syndicated copies of a single initial release.

Principle: Higher independent corroboration increases confidence; wire syndication is tracked separately.
compare_arrows

3. Framing Difference

A low-weight comparative signal measuring coverage variance across outlets reporting the same event.

Explicit Note: This does not judge political bias; it strictly measures comparative textual emphasis between independent reports.
schedule

4. Originality

Identifies the earliest verifiable timestamp of a reported story in the SourceTrace index versus subsequent syndicated copies or secondary reports.

Principle: Establishes "First Observed in SourceTrace" status to credit primary reporting.
label

Rule-Based Claim Classification

SourceTrace attaches a neutral, plainly-worded Claim Type tag to indexed story clusters:

FACT Standard news reporting
DISPUTED Contested by subjects
ALLEGATION Unverified claims
OPINION Editorial / Op-Ed commentary

Disclaimer: Claim classification is an automated rule-based heuristic, not a definitive editorial judgment.
